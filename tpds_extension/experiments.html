FitCachePP TPDS Extension — Experiments

How to read this page. FitCachePP is one system, not three separable features. So we validate it that way. Part 1 measures the whole system — all three mechanisms active together — and asks two questions: (§1.1) what does combining them buy at 1–16 nodes, and (§1.2) does it still hold at 32–256 nodes. We also show one job where the system fills itself organically and survives a mid-job server restart. Part 2 then ablates each mechanism (turns it off, or compares it against the naive alternative) to attribute the whole-system result to the right cause. Whole-system first, component attribution second.

The three mechanisms are causally chained, which is why they have to be tested together:

  1. Transparent mmap cache path delivers the speedup — but only if the cached copy is on the reader's own node, because the win comes from page-faulting directly out of local storage with no network hop.
  2. Keeping each node's copy local is exactly what access-aware (node-local) placement does — without it, a node's files scatter across the cluster and warm reads turn into remote/fallback misses.
  3. And the cache only stays useful across a job requeue or a service crash if the server can rebuild its state, which is what .meta-file recovery provides — without it, a restart makes the cache look empty and everything falls back to a cold PFS read.

Companion pages: EXPERIMENTS_STATUS.html · design.html · implementation_walkthrough.html · paper.html. Every performance cell passes a hard FitCachePP-vs-Native_mmap_PFS checksum-equality gate (per-rank for the multi-seed GNN bench), and a decode gate where applicable.

Part 1 — The whole system (all three mechanisms ON), at two scales

1.1 — Combined result at 1–16 nodes (all three workloads) DONE

This is the small/medium-scale "what do all three mechanisms together buy you" question. It comes in two parts: (a) the current-binary re-run that will stitch onto §1.2, and (b) the original sweep that first established the three workload classes.

How the three walled native cells were recovered. Frontier caps ≤91-node jobs at 2 h (Bin 5). A few 1–16 natives hit that wall on their first attempt: at small N a node cold-reads the entire replicated working set (Megatron 640 GB, DINOv2 541 GB) from Lustre with little read parallelism, and afternoon Lustre contention pushed Megatron N=1/N=2 and DINOv2 N=1 past 2 h. This is the same phenomenon as DINOv2's intractable N=256 native (§1.2) — cold reads of large replicated sets are infeasible within scheduler limits at the extremes, which is exactly what the warm cache removes. A native-only recovery (no warm/stage/server overhead → the full 2 h for the cold read) refilled all three: Megatron N=1 = 1305 s, Megatron N=2 = 3835 s, DINOv2 N=1 = 1045 s, each with per-shard checksums matching the warm arm. The natives are strongly time-of-day dependent (Megatron N=4 native ran in 21 min in the evening vs N=1 walling at 2 h in the afternoon, and N=2's recovery still took 64 min on a later slow patch) — concrete evidence of the "native-volatile / warm-stable" point.

The whole system also runs itself — and survives a restart. Beyond the warm/native tables, one integrated Megatron N=16 job (4819857) drives all three mechanisms together with zero manual staging: the cache fills organically from Lustre (populate 346.5 s), node-local placement keeps every node's copy local (local=160/node, remote=0 — 2560 placements aggregate, zero remote across the 16 ranks), then a metadata server is killed and restarted mid-run — restore_from_sidecars rebuilds 40/40 entries in 5 s, the resolver warm-hits all of them (cache_hit=40, fallback=0), and the post-restart warm pass (209.8 s) is 22.8% faster than the reference warm-hit, with a checksum identical to populate. 1.2 — Combined result at 32–256 nodes (all three workloads) — the headline scaling result DONE

This is the load-bearing result: the full system (mmap path + node-local placement active), current binary, run at 32, 64, 128, and 256 nodes (256 nodes = the 2048-GPU node scale of the base end-to-end runs). Regime-pinned — the working set is replicated per node so it exceeds the 512 GiB node RAM and reads cannot hide in page cache — with mem_hog eviction before each rep. The headline: the warm read time stays flat across the whole range. The client-local resolver adds no coordination cost as the job grows. The native PFS baseline, by contrast, is large and volatile (it rises with Lustre contention but is non-monotonic in N), so the speedup is big at every scale without being a clean function of node count.

Read this as "warm-flat + native-volatile," not "speedup grows with scale." The native cold epoch is non-monotonic in N (Megatron 2568→944→1066→2915 s; GNN 575→441→759→971 s) because it is dominated by time-of-day Lustre contention, not node count. The robust claim is the flat warm read; the speedup is large (single-digit to >10×) at every scale but should not be presented as a monotonic law.

Gates & honesty. Megatron and DINOv2 pass the full checksum gate at every completed rung (Megatron all 17267459847735986; DINOv2 all 66993673013083, warm+native). The GNN rungs report a cosmetic "FAILED": the pooled gate takes a set-union of per-rank checksums, and because each rank samples different random rows by design the union has N distinct values. We verified per-rank that the FitCachePP warm read and the native read are byte-identical for every rank at N=32/64/128/256 — the data is correct; only the aggregate gate is mis-specified. The DINOv2 native at N=256 is intractable (cold-reading the 541 GB replicated corpus on 256 nodes is ~138 TB of simultaneous Lustre reads; force-terminated, core dump, never finished an epoch) — itself evidence of why the local-tier path matters at scale; the warm side stayed flat at 220.6 s. Reduction conventions differ by workload and are stated so: Megatron = rank-0 headline epoch, GNN = cross-rank mean of the steady-state rep, DINOv2 = mean of two warm reps; at N≥64 Megatron's per-rank mean runs higher than its rank-0 headline (straggler tails), which we disclose rather than hide. Result base: {megatron,gnn,dinov2}_scale_ladder/.

Part 2 — Ablations: what each mechanism contributes

Each ablation turns one mechanism off (or compares it against the naive alternative) so the whole-system result above can be attributed to the right cause. They are component studies of one system, not separate systems.

2.3 — Cache tier: DRAM (tmpfs) vs NVMe DONE

The data mover is tier-general (DRAM/tmpfs → PMem → NVMe by remaining capacity); the resolver probes the tiers in the same order. The scaling and integrated runs above were served from the per-node NVMe burst buffer (/mnt/bb), which is the correct tier for the sustained warm-hit claim. A DRAM-tier datapoint confirms the path also serves from tmpfs at parity (48 warm hits resolve to /tmp, bit-identical checksum 3453497658530916), but that is a cold-start regime: when the working set fits tmpfs the Native_mmap_PFS baseline page-caches it too and the sustained gap washes out. NVMe is therefore the tier that backs the headline claim. Result base: mmap_dram_tier/.

What remains missing

Part 1 now validates the whole system across the full range on one binary — §1.1 at 1–16 nodes (current-binary re-run complete; plus the legacy sweep and the integrated organic/restart proof) and §1.2 at 32–256 nodes (2048-GPU scale) on all three workloads, the load-bearing warm-flat result. Part 2 attributes that result to its mechanisms via the placement (§2.1), recovery (§2.2), and tier (§2.3) ablations. Open items: the current-binary 1→256 curve is now complete (all three walled native cells were refilled by native-only recovery jobs). The only permanent gap is the DINOv2 native cold baseline at N=256, which is intractable (~138 TB cold Lustre) and will not be obtained — the warm-flat point at N=256 is captured and the native trend is established at N=32/64/128. Deferred items the panel would still reward but that are not required for the current claims: an end-to-end (or measured I/O-fraction) training run per workload class, an over-capacity eviction run, mid-epoch fault injection, and .meta-file restore at ≥100 K entries.
