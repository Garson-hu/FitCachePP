Structure. Read: when the dataset scales with the machine — the realistic large-training regime — the benefit is sustained at every scale up to 2048 GPUs (15–112×), in contrast to the strong-scaling table above where the fixed 1 TiB corpus shrinks into RAM at 1024+ GPUs. The two sides move oppositely: the warm epoch stays nearly flat (0.7 s → 3.7 s; all I/O is node-local NVMe), while the cold-Lustre native epoch is large and grows noisy with concurrent-reader contention (21 s → 145 s across scales; hundreds-to-thousands of ranks issuing random small reads against the shared PFS). Speedup therefore tracks Lustre's contention behavior, not a cache limitation — exactly the Tpfs-contention regime of the performance model. This is the headline scaling result: FitCache++ turns a contended shared-PFS workload into a scale-invariant node-local one.

Variance decomposition (2026-07-19; answers "why does the speedup rise to 256 GPUs then fall?"). The non-monotonic speedup column is a property of the native baseline, not of the cache. Three pieces of evidence from per-rank data plus cold-slot repeats at the two questioned scales (map: megatron_weak_var/): (1) Native slot-to-slot variance is huge. Repeating the 256-GPU native in a fresh ≥8.5 h-cooled slot on the same shard range gave max 30.0 s / p50 23.4 s versus the ladder slot's 137.5 s / 74.5 s — a 4.6× difference for identical work, so the "112×" peak was a heavily-contended-slot artifact (repeat-slot speedup: 16×). The 2048-GPU point, by contrast, reproduced closely (native 46.1 s vs 53.5 s; speedup 12× vs 15×). (2) The native minimum is invariant. The fastest rank sees ~21 s at every scale in every slot (20.8–37.7 s across all 10 native runs) — the quiet-Lustre floor; everything above it is shared-PFS contention (hot OSTs + ambient load), which produces the wide per-rank spreads (e.g. 24→137 s within one 256-GPU run). Note the reader-to-OST ratio is constant by design (the shard window grows with N), so there is no systematic contention growth with scale — only slot noise. (3) The warm side is repeatable and nearly flat. Warm repeats: 256 GPUs max 1.88 s vs 1.23 s, 2048 GPUs max 3.81 s vs 3.66 s. Its mild growth with N is a tail effect (p50 grows only 0.71→1.45 s while max reaches 3.66 s at 2048 GPUs): the epoch metric is the slowest of 2048 ranks, which amplifies the slow node-SSD class. Net: speedup = (noisy shared-PFS numerator) / (stable local denominator); in repeat slots the curve is flat-with-noise (~16× and ~12× at the two questioned scales). The honest paper statement is the warm side's scale-invariance plus native ranges across slots, not any single speedup peak.

1.5 — Method notes & caveats

Cold baselines. A native measurement is only “cold” if it is the first reader of the corpus after an ≥8.5 h cool-down (Lustre's servers cache recently-read data). Every speedup in §1.1–1.2 is measured that way, one scale per slot; GNN (§1.3) uses 39-day-aged data + page-cache eviction. Cold points are single-shot (a cold measurement consumes its own coldness).
Node-SSD variance. Frontier nodes have two SSD speed classes (~1.9 vs ~5 GB/s direct-read); random-access warm reads are slower on the majority class, and the epoch metric (slowest rank) includes the slowest node. This is a property of node-local storage, not of FitCachePP — worth one discussion paragraph in the paper.
Footprints are exact 1/N. Byte-exact cache census (no growth through warm) and du-verified series (515G→4.0G, 1.0T→8.0G). Table values are per-node du from each run.
Synthetic bytes, real loaders. The benchmark measures the access pattern of real loader code; Megatron reads through its real .idx. Client fixes shipped during the campaign (local warm resolve without PFS stat; open-RPC skip when cached; fstat coverage guard) — warm-path correctness verified at scale: 100% cache hits, zero fallbacks.

Syscall campaigns (§1.1c–d). Timing. CosmoFlow epoch time is the trainer's own per-epoch wall on rank 0 (all ranks agree within 1–5%; slowest rank reported separately); DeepCAM uses MLLOG epoch_start/stop. Cold slots. Every scale reads its own never-read window range; a repeat re-reads the same windows only after ≥9.25 h. RPC robustness. The final clients time out a lost RPC after 20 s and fall back to the PFS instead of hanging (this class of hang — untimed waits after a lost Mercury response at epoch boundaries — cost ~12 jobs across both campaigns before the fix); per-rank counters are logged at exit and are zero on all CosmoFlow ranks, 0–2 ranks up to 256 ranks and 5–23 of 512 ranks in DeepCAM. Probe. The probe's first ~120 s read its own cold corpus and are not a baseline; the 86 s pre-native sleep therefore yields no idle baseline in campaign jobs — the warm-epoch window (job idle on the PFS) is used as the ambient reference instead. Slot variance. Native cold epochs vary up to 3.6× between repeats at ≥512 GPUs (Sep 1–2 post-maintenance Lustre slowdown included); warm epochs vary <2%. Medians of three are reported with the spread. Cache footprint. CosmoFlow's 44.5 GB/node lands entirely in the 100 GiB DRAM tier (NVMe path 40 B); DeepCAM's 97.9 GB/node is NVMe-primary (du-verified 93.9–97.6 GB/node, DRAM ≤1 GB).

Part 2 — Design-feature validations

Part 1 measures the whole system; Part 2 validates the individual design features the system relies on — restart resilience via .meta recovery, and tier generality of the cache path — each with its own measured result. (Design rationale: design.html; code: implementation_walkthrough.html.)

2.1 — Restart resilience: server-independent warm path + .meta recovery RE-VERIFIED 2026-07-07

Design feature: training must survive a cache-service crash/restart. Two layers provide it: the client warm path never depends on server state (deterministic resolve + local verification), and the server rebuilds its bookkeeping from .meta sidecars on startup. Restart test (kill the cache service mid-run, restart, continue the loader):

Scope (honest): the prefetch/populate path used by the headline experiments lands cached shards without .meta files (measured: 31 shards cached, 0 sidecars), so server-side restore_from_sidecars currently rebuilds bookkeeping only for shards promoted through the on-demand (open-triggered) path, which writes sidecars. The earlier restore-count/time tables (old workloads through that path) are retired. Open decision: have the populate path write sidecars too (small server-side change → full recovery coverage), or keep the recovery claim scoped to on-demand-promoted data. Client-side warm correctness across restart holds either way, as measured above.

2.2 — Tier generality: DRAM (tmpfs) vs NVMe DONE

Design feature: the cache path is tier-general — the data mover places by remaining capacity (DRAM/tmpfs → PMem → NVMe) and the resolver probes the same order, so the mechanism is a tiered node-local cache, not an NVMe-only cache.

Why NVMe backs the headline: the DRAM datapoint is a cold-start regime — once a working set fits tmpfs, the native baseline page-caches it too and the sustained gap washes out (same-RAM-pages argument, walkthrough §3b). NVMe is the tier where the >RAM working sets that need the cache actually live.

2.3 — Adaptive placement: automatic 1× vs N× NEW 2026-07-15

Design feature: fitcache_adaptive_decide() chooses the placement strategy at populate time from (a) whether the loader declares a partition, (b) dataset size (one stat/file), and (c) the observed local NVMe budget — instead of the launcher hard-coding 1× (Megatron/Vision) vs N× (GNN). Design: design.html Mechanism 2b; code: walkthrough §4e. The warm hot path is untouched, so all Part-1 results carry over as the performance of whichever strategy the decision selects. 2.4 — CXL/famfs tier on ARC: CosmoFlow 1→16 GPUs vs the published PMem bars DONE 2026-10-23 SUPPLEMENTARY

Design feature: the memory tier is not tied to PMem. On ARC's CXL node the tier is a famfs file system on CXL memory, and each cache server keeps its tier in one pooled famfs file. This is a supplementary result: the IPDPS PMem results stay as published.

Setup. Real CosmoFlow training, train_61440 (61,440 train + 1,024 validation files), batch 2, 5 epochs (1 cold + 4 warm), every run a cold start. Cache: 8 fitcache_server processes on the CXL node c28 (4 Micron CXL devices, famfs on /dev/dax2.0, 512 GB), each with a 32 GiB pool file; transport ofi+tcp over 100 Gb/s IB. Clients: one RTX 4060 Ti per node. The client CPU class is matched to the IPDPS runs at each scale, because CosmoFlow's step time follows the CPU class. 3 runs per scale (6 at 4 GPUs), all counted. Reference: the published IPDPS ARC bars (fig:small_ARC). Reading: the CXL tier is 0.8–4.7% faster than the PMem bars at every scale. Every CXL mean lies inside PMem's 95% CI, and the gaps are the size of the run-to-run spread (CV 0.5–4.1%, 9.8% at 16 GPUs). The claim the data supports is parity with PMem, not a speedup over it.

16 GPUs — node mix: IPDPS used 10 siena + 6 rome nodes. rep2 has exactly that mix and lands at 2,174.8 s (+0.1% vs PMem). rep1 (2 rome) is +1.3%. rep3 has no rome nodes, because rome was excluded after client memory grew on the rome kernel until ranks were OOM-killed, and it is −15.4%. So the −4.7% mean comes from the node mix, not from the tier.

4 GPUs — two node sets: c50–c53 became RTX 5060 Ti nodes after rep1–3, so rep4–6 ran on c2, c3, c54, c55 (c3 has a 2.5 Gb/s IB link). The warm step is 128 ms in both sets. rep1–3 average 5,300.6 s and rep4–6 average 5,081.4 s. All six are reported.
8 GPUs — eighth rank on c28: only seven turin RTX 4060 Ti nodes remain, so c28's own RTX 4060 Ti 8 GB (Turin-family EPYC) takes the eighth rank next to the 8 servers. c0, c1 and c3 have 2.5 Gb/s IB links. IPDPS used c50–c56 and c58.
BeeGFS/HVAC columns are the 2025 IPDPS measurements. A controlled 4-node run on 2026-10-08 (c52, c55, c56, c58; n_train 8192 × 2 epochs) gave the same warm epoch for the CXL pool, a DRAM tier and plain BeeGFS (170–173 s): on an idle BeeGFS the step is bound by compute and the input pipeline. The 20–40% lead over BeeGFS is therefore against the 2025 BeeGFS, not against a BeeGFS run on the same day.
Other differences from IPDPS: FitCache++ code (Frontier branch) instead of the IPDPS build; 8 servers on one CXL node instead of 2 servers per GPU on the two PMem nodes (c35, c36); the first 61,440 train files in tar order (same count, possibly different samples). Every run's server logs show the CXL pool was set up (no fallback). Runs not counted, and why: methodology §12.11.
